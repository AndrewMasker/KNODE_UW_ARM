I updated some stuff to allow easier visualiztion. I added the ability to load a model and continue training it.

10 Feb 2026:
I figured out the bug, I had set the datset options time step to some value without actually making the sim run at
that frequency. Things are looking decent, but I still think adding a FE would be good.

11 Feb 2026:
I made some adjustments to the loss function that allow the position and orientation to be used in conjunction to 
generalized states. This seems to make the model better by quite a bit. I also added fourier embeddings but that
did not make the model better, so it is all commented out at the moment. I changed the last activation from a 
vertically scaled tanh to a diagonally scaled tanh, allowing for the scale to be increased without needing weights
that map to the linear region to be adjusted, this also made the model better by quite a bit. I think my real next
next order of business is to fix how I am using the eqx.field(static=True). I think this is a very large reason 
why the models take forver to compile and trace, as this apparantly takes all of the fields marked static and bakes 
them into the xla code. So I will have to use the partition method to make fields non trainable.

16 Feb 2026:
Fixed thing I mentioned above. Came to conclusion that FE is not good. Figured out that the lower the batch size the
better the training, likely due to the stochasticisty. Figured out that the model trains faster, better, and is less 
likely to freak out and die if I stick to a single layer. Very nice. At this point everything is like 90% there,
it just won't go that last 10%. I think that the main issue is that the network gets saturated figuring out all of the
large features and getting the trajectories to match most of the way, but then gets stuck in a local minimum or it
legit doesn't have the expressivity (which I doubt somewhat). I think that the best way to fix this is to train a 
network then introduce another network to fix the errors in that, ad infinitum.

17 Feb 2026:
Training a network over top of another does not work. I realized that the output scaling factor was set as an int and 
as such was not training. Fixed that and it messed everything up. I just got rid of it and everything is back to normal.
Ran a few tests with different configurations of what is considered in the loss term, qpos, xpos, xquat. The general 
consensus is that it doesn't totally matter but qpos+xpos is the best so far. I believe that many of my issues stem
from not considering velocity within the loss term. I have come to this conclusion from watchin the learned vs ground
truth trajectories, where the main differences would very likely be fixed if the velocities were more informed.
I think that will be my task for tomorrow. I do think as it is we are seeing a considerably better performance than
the nominal model, which may mean that we are good enough, but I would like to continue to adjust until I feel satisfied.
